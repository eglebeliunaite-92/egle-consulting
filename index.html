What's actually breaking

On the surface it looks like a speed problem. Under that, it's something else.

Building got cheap. Your team can prototype a feature in a day, and so can every competitor. There are more products out there than ever, and most of them won't last. The ones that do are built by teams who know who they're building for.

That's where it breaks. Commercial hears from clients every day, but it lives in people's heads or sits behind sales. No one captures it. No one turns it into something the team can build from. So the product gets fixed or launched on assumptions and whoever is loudest.

Delivery has its own problem. The strategy is set, but the team keeps asking each other what it is. Work moves slowly, gets reworked, and lands late. So you step in. You prototype. You make calls that shouldn't be yours. It holds for a while, and then it shows.

A redesign won't fix this. Faster tools won't either. They just get you to the wrong product sooner.

How I work

From what clients say, to what your team ships.

Most engagements start with one product or one feature. They usually touch more than one of these, because they don't come apart cleanly. Evidence with no delivery behind it sits in a deck. Delivery with no evidence ships the wrong thing faster.

Pillar One

Client Evidence

For when you're fixing or launching a product and can't say what it's built on.

I run client interviews, pull together what commercial already knows, and turn it into source-of-truth artifacts the team builds from. Every product decision gets something real behind it, and you have proof you can take to the board.

Pillar Two

Product Delivery

For when the strategy is set, but shipping is slow and fragile.

I partner with your delivery team on one product or feature and rebuild the cycle around it: understanding the problem, deciding, building, shipping, and learning. That includes a clear-eyed review of where AI tools help your team ship and where they just add noise. Once it works on one feature, the team runs it on the next.

Pillar Three

Team & Roles

For when the team you have isn't set up for how products get built now.

Product manager, designer, analyst, product builder. The roles are shifting, and nobody's job description has caught up. I help you decide whether to upskill or hire, define the roles the product needs, and coach your team lead through the change. It starts with the team on this product, not a company-wide restructure.

Questions you're probably asking

Let me answer

Is this coaching or done-with-you?

Done-with-you, mostly. I work inside your team on the product and build the actual thing: research, delivery cycle, roles. Coaching your team lead is part of it, but I'm not here to advise from the sidelines.

Will you design or build the product yourself?

No. Your team builds it. I make sure they're building the right thing, and that they have what they need to ship it.

How much of my time does this take?

Less as it goes. Early on we talk often. The point is to reach a place where the team ships without you making every call, and eventually without me.

I already hired a UX lead. Does that conflict?

No. Often the lead is capable and just doesn't have the client evidence or the process to lead yet. I work with them, not around them.

How fast will I see something?

You get a report from the first call. Then we start on one product or feature, so the first change shows up in something your team ships, not in an org chart.

Can you work with the team I already have?

Yes. I start with the people you've got and the product in front of them. Replacing everyone is rarely the answer.

What if I'm not sure what I need?

That's what the call is for. You don't need a clean brief. You need someone whose job it is to look.

Why not just hire a permanent leader?

You might, eventually. Bringing me in first means you learn what the product needs from the role before you commit a headcount to it.
